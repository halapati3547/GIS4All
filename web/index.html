Why finding land is hard

California has passed laws that let affordable homes be approved without a public hearing on land that used to be off-limits. The laws are public. The parcel data is public. Nobody has joined the two for a small nonprofit.

  • 1
    Sites arrive by luck.A city calls about surplus land, or a church offers its parking lot. There is no list to work from.
  • 2
    Every law has its own tests.Zoning, lot size, flood zones, fire zones, nearby industry, who owns it. One yes-or-no answer needs five or more map layers.
  • 3
    One person, no GIS team.A Habitat acquisitions lead checks each parcel by hand, over weeks, and still does not know if the owner would sell.

What Site Scout does

Three steps, all from public data, every answer showing where it came from.

Step 1 · Screen

Every parcel in the city

Joins zoning, current land use, flood, fire and earthquake zones, and transit for all 42,684 Santa Ana parcels, from county, regional and state data.

Step 2 · Test

Against five state laws

Runs each parcel through the written tests of each law. Every test says pass, fail or "can't check yet", with the source and the date checked.

CLEARUNKNOWNFAIL
Step 3 · Call

A short list to work

Ranks sites from "Strong lead" to "Weak", flags church-owned land, lists what to confirm before calling, and drafts the first call. It never sends anything.

What changes for the acquisitions lead

The person we built for: one Habitat OC staff member responsible for finding the next sites.

Before

  • Waits for a city or a congregation to call.
  • Checks zoning, hazards and the law for one parcel at a time.
  • Weeks per site, no list to compare against.

After

  • Opens a ranked list of sites to call this month.
  • Sees why each site fits and what to confirm first.
  • Types an address a church emailed and gets the same answer in seconds.

Honest about what it does not know

A screen, not an approval. When data is missing, the tool says "unknown" instead of guessing.

Always checked by a person

  • Whether the site is in a historic district.
  • Whether anyone rented it in the last three years.
  • Who really owns it. Church ownership is a lead from the IRS nonprofit list, not a fact.

Assumptions we label

  • How city zoning codes map to each law's categories.
  • The score weights used to rank sites.
  • Distances used to approximate "next to industry".

From one city to all 34

The regional data Site Scout uses already covers every city in Orange County. Adding the next city is one command, plus confirming that city's zoning codes. Each site also exports as data, so a permit-timeline or funding-gap tool can pick up where this one stops.
